Chapter 3: Motion in a Straight Line

OVERVIEW Kinematics: Study of motion without considering the cause of motion (forces). Motion in 1D involves movement along a straight line path (x-axis, y-axis, or vertical free fall).

1. Distance vs. Displacement

Property Distance (\(s\)) Displacement (\(\Delta x\))
Nature Scalar quantity Vector quantity
Definition Total path length covered Shortest distance between initial & final position
Value Always \(\ge 0\) Can be positive, negative, or zero

★ Key Insight: Distance \(\ge |\text{Displacement}|\). Equality holds strictly for unidirectional 1D motion!

2. Equations of Motion (Uniform Acceleration)

IMPORTANT FORMULAS

For constant acceleration \(a = \text{constant}\):

  1. \(v = u + at\)
  2. \(s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2\)
  3. \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\)
  4. \(s_n = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1)\) (Distance in \(n^{\text{th}}\) second)

3. Step-by-Step Solved Problem (JEE Level)

Question: A particle moving with uniform acceleration covers 12 m in the 3rd second and 20 m in the 5th second. Find the initial velocity \(u\) and acceleration \(a\).

Solution:

Using the formula for distance in the \(n^{\text{th}}\) second: \(s_n = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1)\)

  • For \(n = 3\): \(12 = u + \frac{a}{2}(2(3) - 1) \implies 12 = u + \frac{5a}{2}\)     — (Eq. 1)
  • For \(n = 5\): \(20 = u + \frac{a}{2}(2(5) - 1) \implies 20 = u + \frac{9a}{2}\)     — (Eq. 2)

Subtracting Eq. 1 from Eq. 2:

\[ 8 = \frac{4a}{2} \implies 2a = 8 \implies \mathbf{a = 4 \text{ m/s}^2} \]

Substituting \(a = 4\) into Eq. 1:

\[ 12 = u + \frac{5(4)}{2} \implies 12 = u + 10 \implies \mathbf{u = 2 \text{ m/s}} \]
